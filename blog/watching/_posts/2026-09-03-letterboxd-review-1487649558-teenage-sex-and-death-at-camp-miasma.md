 <p><img src="https://a.ltrbxd.com/resized/film-poster/1/1/2/3/8/6/7/1123867-teenage-sex-and-death-at-camp-miasma-0-600-0-900-crop.jpg?v=f6059120f8"/></p> <p>This was the first time I got to watch a film in the new Theater 3 of The Music Box Theatre and *damn* do I love that new setup. You&#039;re so close to the screen and the room is small and everything is lush and new. It feels like you&#039;re in the basement of a Hollywood producer at a private screening.</p><p>I went into this with low expectations. I had enjoyed this director&#039;s debut film years ago, but didn&#039;t enjoy their second nearly as much. If their first film was a statement of category (telling stories of trans life through the lens of horror), then I thought the second was a sophomore album - a self-serious pandering to nostalgia. This third film was a jubilant exhortation of the better halves of those first two: unbridled enthusiasm for filmmaking-as-personal-exploration along with a big bloody bite of juicy horror. And I wholly accepted the director&#039;s joy into my heart.</p><p>I was loving all the music choices. I was loving the beautiful iridescence of the background behind the horror foreground. I was loving the campy candy wrappers under Gillian Anderson.</p><p>Please, more of all of this.</p>
